Privacy

Across counts how it is used so we can make it faster and more reliable. We never see your files, their names, your links or codes, or your IP address in those counts. Here is everything, in plain words.

What we never collect

  • Your file, any part of it, or its name. The file goes straight from one browser to the other, encrypted.
  • The link’s secret or the six-letter code.
  • Your IP address, precise location, or anything that follows you from one day to the next.
  • Cookies, fingerprints, ad IDs, or session recordings. There are no third-party scripts on the page.

What we count

The page sends a short message to our own server when something happens. Our server checks every field against a fixed list, drops anything not on it, and passes the rest to PostHog (in the US), which shows us charts. The list:

WhenWhat is sent
You open a pageWhich page; whether you came from a link, a code or the home page; the name of the website that sent you, if any, and campaign tags like utm_source; your language; your window width
You pick a fileIts size, rounded to two figures (3.4 GB, not 3,412,876,544 bytes); its kind (image, video, document…) and extension if it is a common one like pdf; whether Hide my IP is on
The two browsers connectSender or receiver; link or code; whether it went over the same network, directly, or through the relay; how long connecting took
A transfer starts, finishes or failsSize (rounded) and kind; how long it took and how fast it went; whether compression was used; how the file was saved; if it failed, at what step and a short reason like “disconnected”
Other momentsCopying or sharing the link (not the link itself), a wrong or expired code (not the code), the relay being unavailable, and the text of a page error so we can fix it

With each of these our server adds your country (from the network, not GPS), your browser, operating system and device type (like “Chrome, Windows, Desktop”), and two random labels:

  • A visit label, made fresh each time a page opens and kept only in that page’s memory. It lets us see that one visit picked a file and then connected.
  • A daily visitor label, a scrambled fingerprint of your network address and browser mixed with a secret that changes every day and is then thrown away. It lets us count people per day without cookies. Nobody, us included, can turn it back into an address or link two days together.

Search engines, link previews and other bots are not counted.

Who can see it

Only the people who run Across, in PostHog. We don’t sell or share it, and it isn’t used for ads. PostHog keeps it under its privacy policy, for as long as our plan keeps data.

Turn it off

Nothing is sent if your browser has Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track on. You can also switch it off here, for this browser:

Across works exactly the same either way.

Check it yourself

The code that decides what is sent is open: the page’s track calls in public/index.html, and the fixed list our server enforces in api/event.js. In your browser’s developer tools, the Network tab shows each api/event message as it goes. Questions? Open an issue.
