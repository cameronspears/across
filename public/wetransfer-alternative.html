Across compared with WeTransfer, AirDrop and others

There are three kinds of tools for sending a file. Upload services store a copy for the other person to fetch later. Nearby tools find devices in the same room. Across is a third kind: a direct, encrypted connection between two browsers, anywhere, with nothing stored in between.

Across vs WeTransfer

WeTransfer uploads your file to its servers and emails a link. That is handy when the other person isn’t around yet. But the free plan is small: in November 2026 its help center listed up to 10 transfers or 3 GB in any 30 days, with links that expire after 3 days. Check their site for current limits.

Across never stores the file, so it has no storage plan to sell. A direct or same-network transfer has no size limit from us, and you need no account. The catch is timing: you both need to be online, and your page must stay open until they finish.

Pick WeTransfer when the other person will download it later. Pick Across when they’re around now, the file is large, or you’d rather it never sat on someone else’s server.

Across vs Google Drive, Dropbox and OneDrive

Cloud drives are great for files you want to keep and share for weeks. For a one-off send they’re slow, since the file goes up and then down again, and both of you may need an account. The file also counts against your storage until you delete it. Across sends it once, directly, and keeps nothing.

Across vs AirDrop and Quick Share

AirDrop is excellent between Apple devices in the same room, and works offline. Across fills the gaps: iPhone to Windows, Android to Mac, any device to Linux, and anyone who isn’t nearby. See sending between a phone and a computer.

Across vs Snapdrop and PairDrop

Snapdrop and PairDrop are browser tools that show nearby devices on the same network, and PairDrop can also pair devices to use across networks. Across starts from a link or a six-letter code instead, so it works the same whether the other person is in the next room or another country. It also checks, before any data moves, that the two browsers are really talking to each other and not through someone in the middle, and by default it keeps your IP address hidden from the other person.

What Across doesn’t do

  • It can’t hold a file for later. If the other person is offline, use an upload service.
  • It sends one file at a time. Zip a folder first.
  • It can’t resume a transfer on another day. If the connection drops, send again.
